If your kid plays on dirt Saturday and has an indoor cage session Sunday, you might think you need to buy two pairs of baseball shoes. Check what the cage allows first. If clean sneakers are permitted, your player may already have what they need for Sunday.

The 60-second check before you buy

\- Grass and dirt games. If your league allows molded cleats, start there. The rubber studs are designed to grip the field when your player runs the bases or changes direction. [DICK’S guide to baseball cleats](https://www.kqzyfj.com/click-101620676-17023985?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.dickssportinggoods.com%2Frc%2Fbaseball-softball-cleats-buying-guide)

\- Artificial turf games. Ask the field whether it allows molded cleats or requires turf shoes. If your schedule includes several turf fields, check each venue before assuming the same pair works everywhere.

\- Indoor batting cages. Ask whether the facility allows turf shoes, clean sneakers, or both. If your player already has a pair the cage permits, use those before buying another pair.

Text to send your coach or facility: “My kid has molded rubber cleats. Are those allowed on your field or in your cages? If not, can they wear clean sneakers, or do they need turf shoes?”
