If your player is swinging their drop 10 comfortably, there’s no rush to move to a drop 8 unless their division requires it. If you’re considering the switch, start by comparing two bats of the same length. That makes it easier to tell how the extra weight affects their swing.



 What changes from drop 10 to drop 8?



Bat drop is the difference between the length in inches and the listed weight in ounces. At the same length, a drop 8 is two ounces heavier than a drop 10.



\- A 29-inch drop 10 is listed at 19 ounces. A 29-inch drop 8 is listed at 21 ounces.

\- A 30-inch drop 10 is listed at 20 ounces. A 30-inch drop 8 is listed at 22 ounces.

\- A 31-inch drop 10 is listed at 21 ounces. A 31-inch drop 8 is listed at 23 ounces.



Weight distribution also affects how heavy a bat feels in motion. If you can, compare two drops from the same model line and give your player a chance to swing both.



 How to tell if your player is ready



Start with the bat they already use. Have them take a few normal swings off a tee. Then try the heavier bat at the same length and go back to their regular bat for comparison. If you have access to a demo bat, repeat the comparison with front toss so they have to time a moving ball.



\- Look for repeatable contact. Can they still square up several balls in a row?

\- Watch their timing. Do they have to start noticeably earlier, or are they consistently late with the heavier bat?

\- Watch their swing near the end.  If their first few swings look good and then they start dragging the barrel or losing balance, give them more time with the lighter bat if their rules allow it.



A few swings are a starting point. If the choice is close, try another session before buying. If a heavier drop is required next season, check what your division allows and practice with a legal option ahead of time.
