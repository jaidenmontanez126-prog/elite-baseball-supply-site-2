Two orange knees, a streak down one hip, and another game tomorrow. If your player came home covered in red clay, start with the stained spots before you wash the whole uniform. The quick routine is brush off loose clay, pretreat the marks, wash, and check the knees before drying.





#### 1. Remove the loose clay

Take the pants outside and shake off loose dirt. If the clay has dried into clumps, gently loosen them with a soft brush. Check the cuffs and seams, where dirt can hide even after the knees look better.

#### 2. Pretreat the knees and slide marks

Read the pants’ care label, especially if they have colored piping. Test your stain remover on a hidden spot first. Then apply a product such as OxiClean Max Force Spray directly to the remaining marks and follow the bottle’s directions for how long to leave it on. [OxiClean’s sports uniform cleaning guide](https://www.oxiclean.com/en/stain-solutions/how-to-remove-stains-from-uniforms)

##### 3. Soak the pants if the stains are stubborn

For a pair with clay ground into both knees, an oxygen-based laundry soak such as OxiClean Versatile Stain Remover powder is an option if the care label allows it. Follow the package directions for mixing and soaking, then wash with your regular detergent using the settings on the garment label. [OxiClean’s instructions for soaking baseball pants](https://www.oxiclean.com/en/stain-solutions/how-to-remove-stains-from-uniforms)

##### 4. Check the pants before they go in the dryer

Look at the knees and hip as soon as the wash finishes. If you can still see orange, treat the remaining marks and wash again before drying. If the pants need to be ready by morning, leave enough time for that second wash. [OxiClean’s guide to rewashing stained uniforms](https://www.oxiclean.com/en/stain-solutions/how-to-remove-stains-from-uniforms)

If you’re washing the same pair between tournament games, a spare lets you soak the stained pants without rushing.
