Your child puts on their new baseball glove, tries to squeeze it shut, and barely moves the leather. Before you buy conditioner or spend a week breaking it in, check how the glove fits their hand. A stiff glove can loosen with use. A glove that slips around can still be hard to control after it softens.



### Start with a quick fit check



Have your child put on the glove and open and close it a few times. Watch what happens at their wrist and fingers.



\- The glove stays in place but takes effort to close. Stiffness may be the main issue. Try a few short sessions of catch and see if it starts moving more easily.

\- Their hand shifts or slides out when they squeeze. If the glove has an adjustable wrist closure, snug it up and try again. If the glove still shifts, have them try a youth model with a smaller hand opening.

\- They can close it around a ball but struggle to squeeze it empty. Try a few easy tosses. Watch whether they can hold on to the ball and reopen the glove to take it out.

If you’re still within the return window and the glove feels too big, settle the fit question before applying conditioner or working the leather.



### Break it in with short games of catch



Stand close enough to make soft, accurate tosses. Have your child catch the ball, close the glove around it, and take it out with their throwing hand. Keep the first session short. If they’re fighting the glove on every toss, stop and try again later.



At the next session, look for two signs of progress. Can they close around the ball more easily? Can they get it back out without fumbling? Those are the motions they’ll use on the field.



### What if the first game is this weekend?



Try the new glove during warm-ups. If your child can catch and hold routine throws and get the ball out comfortably, they can give it a go. If they’re still struggling, let them play with their familiar glove if it still fits, and keep breaking in the new one during practice. Keep it out of the oven and microwave.



A good sign the glove is game-ready is when your child can focus on the ball without having to stop and adjust the glove between catches.
