Your child can swing their new bat in the living room, but at practice they’re late on pitches they used to hit. Is the bat too heavy, or are they still getting used to it?



Start by comparing it with a bat they already swing comfortably. Watch what happens over several swings. One late swing tells you very little. A pattern that shows up every time they switch bats gives you something to work with.



### Look for changes in timing and control



\- They’re consistently late with the new bat. If they’re on time with their usual bat during the same round of front toss, pay attention to what changes when they switch.

\- Their swing looks different when they try to catch up. Maybe they rush to start, lose their balance, or have trouble finishing the swing the way they normally do. Look for changes you can see across several swings.

\- The first few swings look fine, then they start struggling. Give them a short rest and let them try their usual bat. If both bats start feeling hard to swing, end the test and try again when they’re fresh.



### Compare the bats in two short rounds



Start off a tee. Keep the ball in the same spot and have your child take five swings with their usual bat and five with the new one. Watch for solid contact and a balanced finish.



Then try easy front toss. Keep the tosses as consistent as you can. After a short rest, switch which bat goes first. That helps you see whether the same pattern follows the bat or just shows up near the end of the round.



Ask your child which bat they felt more in control of. If they consistently make better contact and stay on time with their usual bat, stick with it for games and revisit the new one at practice.



### Check length as well as weight



If the new bat is both longer and heavier, your comparison changes two things at once. If you can borrow two league-approved bats of the same length, try those side by side. When comparing a drop 10 and a drop 8 at the same length, the drop 8 is two ounces heavier based on the listed weights.
